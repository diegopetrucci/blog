> The Tablet is Apple’s chance at applying the lessons learned on the iPhone to a device big and versatile enough to be a low-needs user’s only computer, or to be the only computer that a power user brings while mobile (as Gruber suggested) instead of a laptop. It can be the computer that we buy our parents or grandparents without worrying that we’re signing ourselves up for years of painful tech support calls as they “lose” documents by saving them in the wrong folder, think they can’t save any more files because the desktop is full of icons, delete their browsers’ icons and tell us the internet is gone, keep five different antivirus products half-installed, and fill their RAM with programs they never Quit because they just close every window instead and don’t notice the tiny “running” dot in the Dock or know what it indicates.

[Marco.org](http://www.marco.org/318091966)

Oh God, finally someone translates my thought in a post.

Computers weren’t made for home-users at first, they were suitable for scientists, big companies and so on. The concept of the computer hasn’t changed in fifty years, perhaps the only big change was the introduction of a graphic UI. From that day we’ve seen only few minor changes.

Nowdays, to use a computer properly you have to learn lots of notions that most users don’t have time or want to learn. And it’s ok, they are not supposed to. Think of the fridge: you do nothing except putting foods in it. You don’t care at the internal temperature, if the circuits are giving it electricity, you never set the temperature (Actually, I don’t know at what temperature it works and no one tells me “Hey you are a noob, RTFM”).

This isn’t how things should work. Yeah, we are geeks and like to mess with ours computers and it’s fine. Most people don’t and it’s fine. The tablet (I hope) will make their life easier, probably it’s gonna be the FIRST real personal-computer.
